# Feature Specification: Procedural Audio — Sound Effects and Music

**Feature Branch**: `002-audio`

**Created**: 2026-11-19

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description: "Add simple sound effects and background music generated in the browser
(no audio files): effects for tool fire, hits, defeats, telemetry pickup, level-up, new-threat
banners, win and loss; a looping chiptune-style track whose tempo or intensity rises with
difficulty; a mute toggle (M) and separate music/effects volume, off by default until the player
presses Start."

## Overview

Uptime Survivors (feature 001) is silent. This feature adds short synthesized sound effects for the
main game events and a looping minimal techno-pulse music track whose energy rises with difficulty. All
sound is generated by the game itself at run time — the repository contains no audio files — so
the game stays self-contained (constitution C5) and fully original (constitution C1). Players can
mute everything with one key and set music and effects volume separately. Nothing plays until the
player presses Start.

This feature lifts feature 001's "No audio" non-goal. All other 001 non-goals still apply, including
"no persistence": audio settings reset when the page is reloaded.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-11-19

- Q: What overall feel should the music have? → A: Minimal techno pulse — mostly rhythm and bass,
  little melody, staying in the background.
- Q: Should each tool have its own firing sound, or should they share one? → A: Ping and Load
  Balancer share one firing sound; Ansible Playbook's pulse has its own sound; Firewall Rule makes
  no firing sound and uses only the hit sound (9 sound effects in total).
- Q: Should there be a sound when the engineer takes damage? → A: Yes — one simple damage sound
  every time the engineer takes damage (10 sound effects in total).

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Hear the action (Priority: P1)

While playing, the player hears a short, distinct sound for each important game event: their
projectile tools firing, the Ansible Playbook pulse, the engineer taking damage, enemies being hit, enemies being defeated, telemetry being collected, levelling up, a new
threat appearing, and the run ending in a win or a loss.

**Why this priority**: Sound effects are the core of the request and give immediate feedback that
makes the survivors-like loop feel responsive.

**Independent Test**: Start a run with sound on and play for a minute; each listed event produces
its own recognizable sound, and the game plays exactly as before with sound off.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the title screen, **When** it is shown, **Then** no sound plays.
2. **Given** the player presses Start, **When** the run begins, **Then** sound is on and game events
   produce sounds from then on.
3. **Given** a run in progress, **When** each of these events happens — Ping or Load Balancer fires,
   Ansible Playbook pulses, the engineer takes damage, an enemy is hit, an enemy is defeated, telemetry is collected, the engineer levels up, a "New threat" banner
   appears, the run is won, the run is lost — **Then** that event's sound plays.
4. **Given** each of the 10 event types above, **When** a listener hears them one after another,
   **Then** each is distinguishable from the others (different pitch, rhythm, or timbre).
5. **Given** many of the same event in a short time (for example, a horde being hit), **When** the
   sounds would pile up, **Then** the game limits how often that sound repeats so the mix never
   becomes a constant buzz or distorts.

---

### User Story 2 - Music that builds with the danger (Priority: P1)

During a run a looping minimal techno-pulse track (mostly rhythm and bass, little melody) plays. As the difficulty level rises from 1 to 20, the
music gets faster and more intense, so the player can hear the pressure building.

**Why this priority**: The rising track is the second half of the request and reinforces the
difficulty climb that defines a run.

**Independent Test**: Use the shortened-timer test setting; the music starts on Start, audibly
speeds up or gains layers as the difficulty indicator rises, and stops when the run ends.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a run starts, **When** gameplay begins, **Then** the music track starts and loops
   without audible gaps.
2. **Given** the difficulty level rises, **When** each new level begins, **Then** the music's tempo
   is at least as fast as at the previous level, and is clearly faster at level 20 than at level 1.
3. **Given** a new enemy type debuts (levels 4, 8, 12, 16), **When** that level begins, **Then** the
   music gains intensity (for example, an added layer) in addition to any tempo change.
4. **Given** the game is paused or the level-up screen is open, **When** that screen is shown,
   **Then** the music is quieter (ducked) and returns to normal volume when play resumes.
5. **Given** the run ends, **When** the end screen appears, **Then** the music stops and the win or
   loss sound plays.
6. **Given** the player returns to the title screen or starts a new run, **When** that happens,
   **Then** the music restarts from its beginning and lowest intensity.

---

### User Story 3 - Control the sound (Priority: P1)

The player can mute and unmute all sound instantly with the M key, and can set music volume and
effects volume separately from the pause screen.

**Why this priority**: Sound must never be forced on a player (for example, in a workshop room), so
control ships together with the sounds themselves.

**Independent Test**: During a run press M (all sound stops), press M again (sound returns at the
previous volumes); open Pause, change music and effects volume, and hear the change.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** sound is playing, **When** the player presses M, **Then** all sound is silenced and an
   on-screen indicator shows "Sound off".
2. **Given** sound is muted, **When** the player presses M again, **Then** sound returns at the same
   music and effects volumes as before muting.
3. **Given** the pause screen, **When** it is open, **Then** it shows a mute control and separate
   Music and Effects volume controls, each adjustable from 0% to 100% in steps of 10%, operable by
   keyboard and mouse.
4. **Given** the Effects volume is set to 0%, **When** game events happen, **Then** no effects play
   while the music continues (and the reverse for Music at 0%).
5. **Given** the player changed audio settings, **When** they start a new run in the same page
   session, **Then** the settings are kept; **When** they reload the page, **Then** settings return to
   their defaults (no persistence).
6. **Given** the player presses M on the title screen before ever pressing Start, **When** they then
   press Start, **Then** the run starts muted.

---

### Edge Cases

- **Browser blocks sound before interaction**: sound only starts after the Start press, which counts
  as the player's interaction, so the browser does not block it.
- **Sound cannot start at all** (audio unavailable or blocked by the browser): the game plays
  normally and silently, the indicator shows "Sound unavailable", and no error interrupts play.
- **Tab hidden or window loses focus**: the game already pauses; all sound is silenced until the
  player returns, then music resumes ducked on the pause screen.
- **Several level-ups queued at once**: one level-up sound plays, not one per queued level-up.
- **Win and loss**: exactly one end-of-run sound plays, matching the outcome.
- **Replay Seed / New Run**: music restarts from the beginning; no sounds from the previous run
  continue.
- **Very high event rates** (300 enemies being hit): effect limiting keeps the game's frame rate at
  the level required by feature 001 (SC-005).
- **Reduced motion preference**: does not change sound (it concerns motion, not audio).

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Sound effects**

- **FR-001**: *(Extended by feature 004 with `incidentAlarm` and `incidentResolved` for Major Incidents —
  12 effects in total; see [specs/004-major-incidents/](../004-major-incidents/spec.md).)* The game MUST play a distinct sound effect for each of these 10 events: projectile tool
  fire (shared by Ping and Load Balancer), automation pulse (Ansible Playbook), engineer damaged
  (every time Uptime is lost, whatever the source), enemy hit, enemy
  defeated, telemetry collected, engineer level-up, new-threat banner, run won, run lost. Firewall
  Rule has no firing sound; its contacts use the enemy-hit sound.
- **FR-002**: Sound effects MUST be short (each 1 second or less, except the win and loss sounds,
  which MAY be up to 3 seconds).
- **FR-003**: Each effect type MUST be rate-limited so that it plays at most once per minimum
  interval (default 60 ms for projectile tool fire, hit, defeat, and pickup), and at most 8 effects MAY sound at
  the same time; excess effect requests are dropped, never queued.
- **FR-004**: Several level-ups gained at the same moment MUST produce a single level-up sound.

**Music**

- **FR-005**: A looping music track in a minimal techno-pulse style — mostly rhythm and bass, with
  little or no melody, sitting behind the sound effects — MUST start when a run starts and loop
  seamlessly while the run lasts.
- **FR-006**: Music tempo MUST never decrease as difficulty rises, and MUST be at least 30% faster at
  difficulty level 20 than at level 1.
- **FR-007**: Music intensity MUST increase at each enemy debut level (4, 8, 12, 16) — for example by
  adding a musical layer.
- **FR-008**: Music MUST be ducked to about half its volume while the game is paused or the level-up
  screen is open, and MUST return to full volume when play resumes.
- **FR-009**: Music MUST stop when the run ends, and MUST restart from the beginning at the lowest
  intensity for every new run.

**Controls and defaults**

- **FR-010**: No sound MUST play before the player presses Start for the first time.
- **FR-011**: After Start, sound MUST be on by default with Music at 60% and Effects at 80%, unless the
  player muted it beforehand.
- **FR-012**: Pressing M at any time MUST toggle mute for all sound, and the current state MUST be
  shown on screen ("Sound on" / "Sound off") and announced to assistive technology.
- **FR-013**: The pause screen MUST offer a mute control and separate Music and Effects volume
  controls (0–100% in 10% steps), usable with keyboard and mouse.
- **FR-014**: Audio settings MUST last for the page session (across runs) and MUST reset on page
  reload.
- **FR-015**: The title screen's controls reminder MUST mention the M key.

**Integrity**

- **FR-016**: All sound MUST be generated by the game at run time; the repository MUST contain no
  audio files, and no audio MUST be downloaded from any other site (constitution C1, C5).
- **FR-017**: Sound MUST NOT change gameplay: with the same seed and inputs, a run MUST produce the
  same outcome with sound on, muted, or unavailable (constitution C5).
- **FR-018**: All sound design values (tempos, volumes, rate limits, event-to-sound mapping, music
  patterns) MUST be stored in editable game data and validated with the rest of the game data before
  play (constitution C6).
- **FR-019**: No information MUST be conveyed only by sound; every sounded event keeps its existing
  visual feedback.
- **FR-020**: If sound cannot be started, the game MUST continue silently and show "Sound
  unavailable" instead of the sound indicator.

### Non-Goals

- No audio files, recorded samples, or voice.
- No persistence of audio settings between page loads (feature 001 non-goal still applies).
- No per-tool or per-enemy unique sounds beyond the 10 event types (Ping and Load Balancer share the
  firing sound; Firewall Rule has none; all enemies share "hit" and "defeated").
- No music track selection, multiple songs, or a sound test menu.
- No positional or stereo-panned audio.
- No audio settings on the title screen beyond the M key.

### Key Entities

- **Audio Settings**: muted (yes/no), music volume (0–100%), effects volume (0–100%); held for the
  page session.
- **Sound Effect Definition**: event type, sound shape (pitch, duration, timbre), minimum repeat
  interval.
- **Music Definition**: note patterns, base tempo, tempo at each difficulty level, layers and the
  difficulty level each layer joins, duck level.
- **Audio State**: whether sound has started, whether it is available, current music intensity.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: Sound begins within 0.5 seconds of pressing Start, and nothing is heard before Start.
- **SC-002**: Pressing M silences all sound within 0.1 seconds, and pressing it again restores the
  previous volumes.
- **SC-003**: In a listening test, a reviewer correctly matches the 10 effect sounds to their events
  at least 9 times out of 10.
- **SC-004**: Music tempo at difficulty 20 is at least 30% faster than at difficulty 1, and never
  decreases between levels.
- **SC-005**: With sound on and 300 enemies on screen, gameplay stays as smooth as feature 001 requires
  (no visible stutter; same frame-rate result as 001 SC-005).
- **SC-006**: The same seed and inputs produce identical runs with sound on, muted, and unavailable
  (100% of determinism test repeats).
- **SC-007**: The repository contains zero audio files, and a full run makes zero network requests
  to other sites.
- **SC-008**: At least 3 of 5 playtesters rate the sound as adding to the game, and none describe it
  as harsh or annoying after a 5-minute session.

## Assumptions

- "Off by default until the player presses Start" means silence on the title screen, with sound
  starting on Start. Starting muted is still possible by pressing M before Start.
- Default volumes (Music 60%, Effects 80%) and the 60 ms repeat limit are starting values to tune by
  ear; they live in game data.
- The engine already reports most needed events (hit, defeat, pulse, engineer damaged, level-up, difficulty change,
  run end); "projectile fired" and "telemetry collected" signals may need to be added to the engine's
  event list without changing gameplay.
- The pause screen is the natural place for volume controls; adding a separate settings screen is
  out of scope.
- Desktop browsers with keyboard remain the target, as in feature 001.
- This feature amends feature 001's non-goal "No audio"; feature 001's spec should note that audio is
  delivered by feature 002.
